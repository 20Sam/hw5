The plan got the who right: facilities only on 102, inventory never on 102, and the forbidden tools (a second payment, a purchase order to a blocked vendor) never called. It got the when wrong. I pictured a boss who asks one specialist, reads the answer, then asks the next. Pydantic AI let the boss fire three ask_teammate calls in one step, so it ran a parallel kickoff every time (same UTC second on all three tickets). That made each ticket fast (37 s, 32 s, 50 s), but customer service always started before the facts it needed existed. It covered the gap by asking a specialist itself (101, 102) or by writing around it (103). The one prediction that landed to the cent was money: the plan said $160 would be left after 101 and 102, and the Cash tab ends at $160.00.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Ticket 102, almost certainly. One agent holding get_rent_due, get_cash_balance, request_payment, save_customer_draft and set_ticket_status could do it in about five tool calls: read lease 1, check cash, request $2,400, draft the acknowledgement, resolve. The team used 21 model calls and 57,107 tokens. The evidence that the extra agents added little: the boss itself called get_rent_due, get_cash_balance, list_payment_requests and list_drafts, and facilities verified the same lease twice.

Ticket 101 too. It's a straight line: stock (0) → why (invoice 501 blocks Bulldog Print Co) → request the invoice → draft the alternatives. The boss already re-checked stock, the invoice hold and the cash itself; inventory, accounting and customer service mostly repeated those reads. My rough estimate is a quarter of the tokens for the same outcome.

Where the team earned its keep is 103, and in guardrails more than brains. The split of duties is real safety: only accounting may request money, only the boss may change a ticket's status, customer service can't see cash at all, and the approval tools exist for no agent. A single agent would need the human gate and the tool-level rules to carry all of that (they do: the amount comes from the database, $0 is a floor, drafts can't mention internal terms). My takeaway for Campus Customs: one worker agent for routine tickets (rent notices, stock questions), and the team only when a ticket crosses money, stock and a customer at once, with the boss asking in order rather than all at once.

5. Three problems this team could not resolve, and what it would need

  1. Taking Yale AI Club's money. The boss approved 20 hoodies at $49.30 ($986.00), but this shop can only spend. There is no sales order, no invoice to a customer and no way to record money coming in (“cash only goes out” is literally how the data works). Needed: a sales_orders table and tools create_sales_order, send_customer_invoice (as a draft) and record_payment_received (human-confirmed), plus a Sales agent that turns a decided price into an order and tracks the deposit. Accounting would then forecast with income, not just bills.
  2. Receiving and delivering stock. When Bulldog Print Co's reprint lands “as early as 2026-09-05”, nothing changes inventory.qty, and the shop date never moves past 2026-08-31, so the team can't even see the day arrive. Nobody can book QuickShip CT (vendor 3, the courier) either. Needed: advance_shop_date (or a scheduler), receive_shipment(po_id) that adds stock when an approved order arrives, reserve_stock(ticket, sku, size, qty) so the 8 hoodies aren't sold twice, and book_courier, all run by a Fulfillment agent with human approval on anything that costs money.
  3. October's rent with $160 in the bank. Lease 1 is due again 2026-10-02 for $2,400. request_payment will correctly refuse (“would take checking below $0”), but refusing isn't solving. The team can't forecast cash, can't arrange credit, and isn't allowed to contact the landlord to negotiate. Needed: forecast_cash(days) using sales orders and bills, a Finance agent that proposes a payment plan or a transfer from another account, and a human-approved send_message tool (today every message is a draft that a person sends by hand). It would also need real sign-in on the board, since today anyone at the desk could type any name into Approve.
